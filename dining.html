The Room

The finest room in the house, and it is the one you eat in

Most country houses keep their best room for show. Coul does not. The Octagonal room is genuinely eight-sided, rising to an ornate ceiling eighteen feet above the tables, with a fireplace at one wall and three ceiling-to-floor windows framing Strathconon and the mountains beyond.

In summer the light stays in the room until very late. In winter the fire is lit, the candles go on at four, and the windows turn to black glass with the hills just visible in them. Either way, nobody rushes you.

The Kitchen

A contemporary menu, cooked from what is close

Our kitchen holds one AA Rosette and is working towards a second. The cooking is contemporary and unfussy, built on Highland produce — and the wine list rewards a second look.

From the water

Scotland's west coast is ninety minutes away, and the east coast ports rather less. What arrives is what we cook.

From the hill

Venison, beef and lamb from the estates and farms around us, hung properly and treated simply.

From the garden

Roots, leaves and soft fruit in their short Highland season, from growers we can name.

Lunch

Lighter, and rather good

Lunch is served in the Octagonal room and the bar. Choose from the main menu, or from a sandwich menu that is a good deal more interesting than that description suggests — the right thing before an afternoon on the hill.

Afternoon Tea

Scones, still warm

Taken in the Octagonal room or by the drawing room fire, and built around what you actually want: choose your own sandwiches, then warm and light scones with clotted cream and homemade jam.

It is a fine way to spend a wet Highland afternoon, and a better one to spend a dry one on the lawn.

The Bar & Drawing Room

Where the evening goes after dinner

The bar keeps a proper Highland selection, and the drawing room keeps an open fire. Coffee comes through, someone finds the good armchair, and an hour disappears without anyone deciding it should.

Dogs are welcome here, as they are everywhere in the house except the restaurant itself.

  • Highland and Speyside malts, with Glen Ord a short drive away
  • A wine list with genuine depth, and advice if you want it
  • An open fire in the drawing room from autumn through spring
Dining With Us

Non-residents very welcome

You do not need to be staying to eat here — but the Octagonal room is not large, so it is worth booking, particularly at weekends and through the summer. Tell us about allergies, and about anything you would rather not be served, and the kitchen will work around it.
