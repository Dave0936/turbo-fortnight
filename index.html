A Welcome Two Centuries in the Making

A house built for gathering, and for staying a while

Coul House was raised in 1821 for the Mackenzies of Coul, and it has been giving people a reason to linger ever since.

Beyond the gravel sweep, the door opens onto tall windows, an open fire in the drawing room, and the particular hush of a house that has always known how to look after people. Fires are lit before you arrive. Boots are dried overnight. Nobody hurries you through breakfast.

Stuart and Susannah Macpherson run Coul as a family hotel rather than an institution — twenty-one individual bedrooms, a kitchen that cooks what the Highlands give it, and eight acres of mature woodland to walk off the pudding in.

The Octagonal Restaurant · One AA Rosette

Dinner in the finest room in the house

Coul's dining room is genuinely octagonal — eight walls rising to an eighteen-foot ornate ceiling, a fire at one side, and three floor-to-ceiling windows that frame Strathconon and the mountains beyond. At dusk, with the candles lit, there is nowhere better to sit down to dinner.

The kitchen cooks a contemporary menu from local produce, with a wine list that rewards a second look. Afterwards, take your coffee — or something older and peatier — through to the drawing room, where the fire will still be going.

Weddings & Celebrations

A house, a glen, and a day that is entirely yours

Coul House is licensed for civil ceremonies, so you can marry in the Octagonal room beneath its ornate ceiling, on the lawn with the mountains behind you, or among the trees — and then sit down to dinner in the same house your guests will sleep in.

We take one wedding at a time. There is no second party in the next room, and no clock ticking on your evening.

  • Licensed for civil ceremonies, indoors or in the grounds
  • From two guests up to 80 through the day, and 140 in the evening
  • Twenty-one bedrooms, so everyone comes down to breakfast together

Beyond the Door

A very good place from which to see the Highlands

Coul sits on the North Coast 500, twenty-five minutes north of Inverness and within an easy morning of the west coast. Everything worth driving to is a comfortable day out — and you come back to a fire.

Rogie Falls

Salmon on the fish ladder and a suspension bridge over the gorge, two miles away in Achilty Forest.

Hills & Glens

Ben Wyvis on the doorstep, Strathconon behind the house, and forest trails straight from the grounds.

Malt & Distilleries

Glen Ord a short drive south, Speyside within reach, and a well-stocked bar to come home to.

The Grounds

Eight acres of lawn and old woodland, a mini golf course, and somewhere to sit with a book.

Finding Us

Closer than you think

Coul House stands in the hills just above the village of Contin, twenty-five minutes north of Inverness and the last comfortable stop before the road runs west to Ullapool and the coast.

  • Inverness and its airport — around 25 minutes by car
  • Achilty Forest and Rogie Falls — 2 miles, or on foot from the grounds
  • Strathpeffer, the Victorian spa village — 3 miles
  • Free parking in the grounds, and dogs welcome throughout
